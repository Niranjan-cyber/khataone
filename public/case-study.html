Bahi बही · Pixelcraft 2026 · DS-03 · Round 1 case study

Udhaar in one thumb.
Less time on hisaab, more time on dukaan.

A digital khata for kirana shopkeepers: credit and sales, a ledger per customer, polite reminders and UPI collection. Designed for one hand, behind a busy counter, on a budget Android phone.

Live prototype: bahi-kirana.vercel.app · Code: github.com/Niranjan-cyber/khataone

The problem

A regular says “Bhaiya, ₹250 khate mein daal dena” and walks away. The shopkeeper is serving others, goods or cash in one hand. Paper is fast but cannot remind or take UPI. Apps can, but one entry usually means: open customer → find the button → fill a form.

Who it is for

  • Kirana owners, many older or new to smartphones, most comfortable in Hindi or Marathi.
  • Budget Android phones, patchy internet, bright counters, interruptions.
  • Customers they know by name: the relationship matters more than the money.

What we learned (desk research only)

  • Store listings show OkCredit, Khatabook and PhoneKhata already offer ledgers, reminders and UPI. The gap is speed during service, not features.
  • One-handed use: frequent actions belong in the bottom half (thumb zone); targets ≥ 48 px.
  • Low literacy: recognition over recall (faces, names, usual amounts), words next to icons.

Hypotheses from public listings and HCI guidance. No shopkeeper interviews were possible before this round.

Key design decisions

  • Customer first, then amount. Recent customers sit on Home; the keypad opens already addressed to them.
  • Undo, not “Are you sure?” The button repeats the amount (“Save ₹250 udhaar”); a 6 s Undo follows.
  • Never colour alone. ↗ UDHAAR / ↙ JAMA: word + arrow + colour + running balance.
  • Wrong entries are struck through, never deleted, so the khata stays trustworthy.

The critical flow: ₹250 udhaar from the home screen, 5 taps

New customer: “Add udhaar” → search or create by name → same keypad. Ledger, reminder and UPI collect are one tap from each customer.

Visual identity

The red-cloth bahi-khata opened at Diwali, made digital: maroon bookcloth with a gold double rule, a ledger page with a red margin, ruled rows instead of cards. Martel for money and names, Hind for interface text.
cloth gold page udhaar jama

Risk handled: red is the brand colour and also means “owed”, so meaning is always carried by the word and arrow too.

Accessibility & inclusion

  • Full English, Hindi and Marathi; reminder language chosen separately from app language.
  • Amounts 60–88 px; body ≥ 16 px; targets ≥ 48 px; works at 320 px wide.
  • Screen-reader labels, focus rings, keyboard entry, reduced motion; works offline.

Limitations & next steps

  • UPI, payment links and messaging are simulated and marked DEMO; production needs a payment provider with server-side confirmation.
  • Next: time 5–8 shopkeepers (median, range, % under 10 s), Hindi/Marathi copy review by native speakers, cloud backup.
